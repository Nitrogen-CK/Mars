# Warrens v6r1: two-player level-design review

20 October 2026. Reviewed source at `3f075ca7fd9eac0b67d78c6b9c36b7290f2d0d47`. Review only; no map, gameplay, build settings or existing design documents changed.

## Recommendation

**Keep the footprint. Turn this into a compact, readable expedition through a monastery that once fed its dead. Make the Well the central revelation, the kitchen the earned relief, and the loaded return the payoff. Prove one complete two-player contract before adding another district, gate or mandatory ingredient journey.**

The layout already has the ingredients of an unusually good dungeon: several levels around a visible common void, alternative approaches to the main encounter, a secret circulation layer, and shortcuts that change the return journey. Its weakness is the distance between that spatial promise and the actions currently implemented. Several named objects are still boxes; different pockets draw from the same tables; important puzzles may reward nothing; and the current combat composition explicitly excludes joined clients.

**Judgment:** strong dungeon structure; an unfinished encounter and art pass; not yet a validated two-player horror level. The next improvement should make existing places matter. Another extension would mostly increase the surface area of unfinished work.

The user's current strong-horror and D&D direction governs this review. The older GDD says the game is not primarily horror ([GDD.md:40](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/concepts/mise-en-monstre/GDD.md:40)); that is a direction change to acknowledge, not a reason to reject the request. Preserve the established low-poly, graphic food-and-bone style ([VISUAL_BOARD.md:9](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/concepts/mise-en-monstre/VISUAL_BOARD.md:9)). Horror can come from implication, scale, dependence and anticipation without replacing it with realistic gore.

## Evidence and what to preserve

The supporting [route/unlock inventory](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/reviews/2026-10-20-warrens-two-player/ROUTES_AND_UNLOCKS.md), [source receipt](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/reviews/2026-10-20-warrens-two-player/SOURCE_RECEIPT.json) and [static results](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/reviews/2026-10-20-warrens-two-player/route-audit.json) contain the detailed audit.

**Confirmed:** current `v6r1` has 51 graph nodes and 59 links. My independent Dijkstra check covered all 64 permitted route-closure subsets from five entrances: **320 cases, zero connectivity failures**, with a court at least 50 m away in every case. This proves the graph property only. The graph treats `aE -> K` as ordinary walking, while actual K access requires PK shutters ([v6:53](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v6.py:53), [builder:1291](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/build_warrens.py:1291)).

Preserve three structural strengths:

- **One recognizable void at several heights:** Pit -5 m, rim 0 m, Bridge +2.5 m and galleries +5 m. Seeing a destination before reaching it makes the Well more valuable than another isolated room ([v5:159](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v5.py:159)).
- **Discovery improves the loaded return:** closet/SC2 and Chime ladders intentionally accept the worn pack. Keep those rewards generous ([v5:51](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v5.py:51)).
- **The Grotto approaches really differ:** Court/spiral arrives from the north; lower Cistern route from the east; overlook adds information and optional commitment ([v6:55](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v6.py:55)).

**Historical only:** G2's 19/19 tests/seed smokes and the viewed [lighting sweep](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Saved/AnchorPlans/lighting-r1/ingame_fills_sweep.png) / [lamp comparison](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Saved/AnchorPlans/lighting-r1/ingame_lamps_before_after.png). They are not fresh traversal, multiplayer or current-scene evidence. No Unreal build, automation, Warrens play session, physical-input or multiplayer run occurred during this review. The lead made only a read-only editor query, finding CrawlerDoorway open. Proposals below are unimplemented design recommendations unless identified otherwise.

## Ranked blockers and design risks

### 1. Two-player parity and recovery must precede a claim of two-player readiness

**Confirmed composition limit:** `Get_IsLocalCombatWorld` accepts Standalone and ListenServer only and explicitly describes combat as local/non-replicated ([Mars_LocalSession.as:3](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/Common/Mars_LocalSession.as:3)). Player Health/hit-zone construction is gated by that predicate and local control ([Mars_PlayerCharacter.as:380](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/PlayerCharacter/Mars_PlayerCharacter.as:380)); crawler combat uses the same gate ([Mars_Crawler_Utils.as:104](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/ECS/Crawler/Mars_Crawler_Utils.as:104)). This is a direct source reason that a joined player's intended combat experience is not established.

Anchor resolution also depends on an anchored GameMode, with clients explicitly excluded in its contract ([Mars_AnchorResolver_Subsystem.as:1](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/ECS/AnchorResolver/Mars_AnchorResolver_Subsystem.as:1)); route candidates and fixed gates declare `DoesNotReplicate` ([Mars_Anchor_Candidates.as:82](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/WorldObjects/Anchors/Mars_Anchor_Candidates.as:82), [Mars_Gate_EntityScript.as:15](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/WorldObjects/Mechanisms/Mars_Gate_EntityScript.as:15)). **Inference:** candidate population and operated state may differ between machines. The actual symptom still needs a two-process run; this review does not claim observed desynchronization.

**Recovery gap in examined gameplay:** downing holds movement until health/flag conditions clear ([Mars_PlayerCharacter_Hfsm_Combat.as:1](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/PlayerCharacter/HFSM/Mars_PlayerCharacter_Hfsm_Combat.as:1), [line 45](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/PlayerCharacter/HFSM/Mars_PlayerCharacter_Hfsm_Combat.as:45)). The production player-heal call located by source search is in the debugger ([Mars_DebugPage_Player.as:76](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/Debugger/Pages/Mars_DebugPage_Player.as:76)); no teammate revival action was found in the examined paths. Do not design death as a fun rescue beat until it exists. A corpse or pack stranded at the closet ladder can remove half the party's agency.

**Recommendation:** first establish shared population, player damage, gates, key/cargo ownership and one explicit downed-player recovery rule. Required mission progress must remain possible with one active chef. Both players down should produce a clear failure/reset, not an indefinite immobilized session. Rejoining is not an assumed rescue: camp-departed sessions deliberately close admission ([Mars_Gameplay_GameMode.as:12](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/WorldSettings/Gameplay/Mars_Gameplay_GameMode.as:12)).

### 2. The map's named activities overstate its currently playable content

The Grotto crown and vent have strong descriptive notes, but their authoring route is generic prop geometry, not a harvest/vent encounter ([design_warrens_v6_geometry.json:1490](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v6_geometry.json:1490), [line 1505](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v6_geometry.json:1505), [build_warrens.py:1136](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/build_warrens.py:1136)). Chimes are hanging shapes whose lower ends deliberately stay above head height; no alarm behavior follows from that prop ([build_warrens.py:952](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/build_warrens.py:952)). The fresh generated manifest contains **zero functional Station entity rows**; kitchen benches/stations are boxes. The generated Vault contains only its Red door/lock, with **no reward or cache anchor**. These absences are confirmed in generated content; separate hand-placed actors remain uninspected ([content audit:106](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/reviews/2026-10-20-warrens-two-player/ROUTES_AND_UNLOCKS.md:106), [Vault:114](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/reviews/2026-10-20-warrens-two-player/ROUTES_AND_UNLOCKS.md:114)). The selected foyer kit is shared: one pack, one lantern and two tallow; required tools must be explicitly supplied by the acceptance fixture ([kit audit:110](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/reviews/2026-10-20-warrens-two-player/ROUTES_AND_UNLOCKS.md:110)).

**Recommendation:** select one existing quarry/ingredient handling loop and finish its required interactions and return. Give the Grotto a separate contract or an optional useful ingredient before making its trip mandatory in every Pit run. The proposal's crown -> bait -> intact joint chain can become a richer later contract, but requiring it principally to reach 15–20 minutes is the wrong reason. Its own document acknowledges that serial order does not establish duration ([ASTRA_PROPOSAL.md:53](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/design/warrens-expansion-r02/ASTRA_PROPOSAL.md:53), [line 70](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/design/warrens-expansion-r02/ASTRA_PROPOSAL.md:70)).

### 3. Gates need to express different decisions, and a solved optional puzzle needs a dependable payoff

The route table's `Open` role can spawn an arch, a two-sided lever gate or an eligible one-sided gate ([Mars_Anchor_Presets.as:7](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/WorldObjects/Anchors/Mars_Anchor_Presets.as:7)). It does **not** mean its leaf begins raised: the gate default is closed and the two-sided candidate does not override it ([Mars_Gate_Feature.as:36](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/ECS/Gate/Mars_Gate_Feature.as:36), [Mars_Anchor_Candidates.as:100](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/WorldObjects/Anchors/Mars_Anchor_Candidates.as:100)). The older G2 diagnosis of an already-raised useless R3 gate should not be repeated as current fact.

The design concern remains: repeated adjacent levers can become door-opening tax. Treat arches as normal circulation, rubble as a clearly abandoned route, crawl/one-sided gates as scout-and-carrier cooperation, and fixed mechanisms as authored world relationships. Distinguish them visually before interaction.

Bellkeeper currently has two ordered seals, immediate acceptance feedback and a plain-box clue ([build_warrens.py:107](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/build_warrens.py:107), [line 1273](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/build_warrens.py:1273), [Mars_Sequence_Processor_Requests.as:49](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/ECS/Sequence/Mars_Sequence_Processor_Requests.as:49)). It therefore does not currently reward environmental deduction. Four caches compete for a budget of two, with a null dormant row; Bellkeeper and Mourner are among those cache anchors ([design_warrens_v5.py:238](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v5.py:238), [line 296](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/design_warrens_v5.py:296), [Mars_Anchor_Presets.as:29](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/WorldObjects/Anchors/Mars_Anchor_Presets.as:29)). **Confirmed design consequence:** these authored gates are not guaranteed to conceal an active cache.

Finish the already-approved Bellkeeper C1/C3 direction before inventing another puzzle: a real phrase, neutral entry slots, explicit bell commit and visible reward loss with a guaranteed last reward ([PROPOSAL_I.md:16](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/design/warrens-expansion-r02/PROPOSAL_I.md:16)). Keep it optional. Display the consequence before committing and make the clue practical for both a speaking pair and one surviving player. A fixed memorized phrase becomes a later-run ritual; vary the phrase only if repeat-run deduction actually matters. Do not add a long timer to defeat memorization.

### 4. Horror pressure needs a tested rule, not more darkness or more random sentries

The lantern ward is **target-specific**, not an aura protecting the party. It requires the target to hold a lit lantern that overlaps the crawler, within its Shun radius ([Mars_Crawler_Light.as:59](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/ECS/Crawler/Mars_Crawler_Light.as:59)). Targeting retains a keepable current target without testing that ward ([Mars_Crawler_Processor_Targeting.as:36](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/ECS/Crawler/Mars_Crawler_Processor_Targeting.as:36)). **Inference to test:** one chef can hold attention safely while the second performs the valuable action. That could be satisfying brief cooperation or an indefinitely repeatable solution that erases tension.

Do not invalidate the lantern unexpectedly to force fear. Test the actual exchange: how long can the bearer hold, what meaningful choice does the partner make, and when must they trade roles? Use a bounded window or a comprehensible change in encounter state if needed. Avoid designing a map-wide stalker on the assumption that pocket crawlers already pursue through corridors. The current threat anchor home radius is 600 cm ([Mars_Anchor_Presets.as:273](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/WorldObjects/Anchors/Mars_Anchor_Presets.as:273)); a roaming horror encounter is separate work.

Make silence, obscured movement and evidence do part of the job. Thomas Grip's account emphasizes anticipation and how players optimize fear away; that supports testing the ward strategy and leaving space between confrontations, not copying Amnesia's mechanics ([Frictional Games](https://frictionalgames.com/2019-10-9-years-9-lessons-on-horror/)).

### 5. The longer Grotto route needs a reason that survives familiarity

Fresh all-open graph distances, using the authored `costCm` values:

| Walk | Distance | Boundaries |
|---|---:|---|
| K -> Grotto east port through Pump/Cistern | 94.95 m | Ordinary graph links, kitchen treated open |
| Same endpoints through Court/spiral, with Cistern-to-Grotto link excluded | 143.05 m | Alternate route is 48.10 m longer |
| K -> Pit H2 | 73.16 m | Does not include closet ladder, drops or other off-graph shortcuts |
| K -> Chime CH | 60.03 m | All route anchors available |

These are centerline graph costs, not measured walking distances or times. They omit terrain handling, mechanism operation, real pack clearance and optional links. They nevertheless identify a design question: why will an informed carrier choose the Court route after seeing the spiral once?

**Recommendation:** give the Court approach better advance information and the Cistern approach a different relevant resource or bounded encounter cost. Let the overlook reveal the Grotto worksite and its safe return. In one contract the pair values that information; in another they take the direct waterworks route. Do not make every loop equal length. Justin Alexander's dungeon-design account supports interconnections that change tactics, while explicitly cautioning that excessive connections can produce meaningless choice ([techniques](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/13103/roleplaying-games/xandering-the-dungeon-part-2-xandering-techniques), [design tradeoffs](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/13113/roleplaying-games/xandering-the-dungeon-part-3-the-philosophy-of-xandering)).

### 6. District identity should be readable in silhouette and action

The historical sheets show warm/cool separation, but many views still share orthogonal walls, flat ceilings, box landmarks and a repeated grid. That is appropriate for blockout, not evidence of distinct finished areas. Raising district fill intensity will not supply missing shape or environmental history. The current unshadowed fills also reach each named room broadly; use them for the minimum navigation read, while local fixtures establish what deserves attention ([build_warrens.py:84](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Tools/LevelDesign/anchor_plans/build_warrens.py:84)).

**Recommendation:** use four related architectural families with a few exceptional spaces: inhabited kitchen; funerary upper rooms; working waterworks; encroaching natural/living lower spaces. Reuse the same stone kit, but change ceiling profile, orientation, large negative spaces, floor condition and human-scale fixtures. A player should recognize a district from a cropped greyscale image before reading its label.

Clues should let players infer that history from repairs, wear, tools and repeated symbols. Do not explain every room through a note, and do not make every environmental curiosity a switch.

## Mechanism hierarchy

The [exact unlock table](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/reviews/2026-10-20-warrens-two-player/ROUTES_AND_UNLOCKS.md:66) separates every control and effect. Keep a small vocabulary: PK teaches a local persistent release; one-sided route gates support scout/carrier access; P2 teaches a remote connection; P4 rewards observation; P1 rewards vertical exploration; P5/SC2 reward a shorter return. P3 currently accepts one chef or pack and latches, so it cannot honestly be presented as simultaneous cooperation. Give every named optional gate a guaranteed useful reward or clearly visible spent state. Defer a second Drowned Gallery gate.

## A specific two-player run to test first

**Proposed acceptance scenario: “Deep Supper.”** This is a future playable fixture, not a completed run. Use S3 entrance and H2 Pit quarry; guarantee required tools and a real ingredient/cooking endpoint. Set R2 to the Chime-side one-sided lever, R4 to rubble and other main route sockets to arches. The old 15–20 minute target is not this first test's pass condition.

1. **Chime -> Well:** both enter from S3, pass beneath the great hanging instrument, and open R2 from their already-accessible Chime side. Do not invent a crawl challenge here. Give them time to observe the Pit together before choosing an approach.
2. **Claim home:** the closed east cargo door leads them round the public aisle to the west drive. A operates it; B confirms the service shutter moves. Both choose tools and the pack carrier. Keep this discovery short.
3. **Choose the southern detour:** R4 blocks the nearest southwest rim segment. In this run they choose kitchen south -> Pump -> Salt Cellar -> Sluice -> Chime because optional salt improves the order and the wheel opens a fallback path. If no useful salt reward exists, remove that incentive from the brief instead of pretending it does.
4. **Understand the world:** A turns the Sluice wheel; its emblem/local acknowledgement identifies the released Drowned Gallery grate. B loads salt and checks the next approach. No one holds a switch for the other chef's entire trip.
5. **Commit from the east:** `ch1 -> d4 -> H2` compresses the view, then opens into the space they previously saw from above. They identify cargo staging and the closet exit before the encounter. Use an implemented quarry/harvest capability; a concept-named Pickle Mimic, feeding phase or Long Widow is not automatically present.
6. **Exchange useful roles:** A initially holds the lantern while B harvests/loads. Test target retention here: indefinite safe attention holding is a failed encounter test, not proof of successful cooperation. Aim for temporary protection, a handling decision and a voluntary swap/retreat; do not arbitrarily invalidate the light.
7. **Earn the return:** B climbs the closet ladder and opens SC2, then clears the landing for A's worn pack. They return through the public chapel aisle and open east cargo port. Let the learned shortcut pay off.
8. **Make dinner:** one unloads/prepares while the other uses a different station. The same cargo and its condition determine the result; retain the short final-cook direction ([GDD:361](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/docs/concepts/mise-en-monstre/GDD.md:361)). Working preparation, serving and grading are required fixture work.

**Failure branch:** down A with the pack near the Pit ladder. B needs a clear rescue, cargo-recovery or failure/reset rule. Debugger healing does not count. Separately test exhausted light, opposite-direction ladder approaches, dropped ingredients and a dropped optional key.

**Next contracts:** make a Court/Cistern-start Grotto contract prioritize its two approaches and overlook; make a Chime contract prioritize the gallery/ladder return. Do not require all three districts' complete loops in every order. Familiar pairs should form different plans, not merely execute the same circuit faster.

## Rules for a pair

Roles should swap at natural thresholds; brief separation should buy information/access and end at a readable reunion point. Every essential action must remain sequentially solvable by one survivor. Drops and optional penalties need visible consequences before commitment. The kitchen needs an **authored threat-boundary/refuge rule** if it is to promise safety: its shutters latch open, and stationary lights/hooks do not provide the held-lantern ward.

Test the physical pack contract rather than relying on `packFits=false`: worn and bare crouch heights differ, but changing pack state while already crouched affects the next crouch ([Inventory:187](D:/Repositories/CkRepos/Orion/Script/PlayerCharacter/HFSM/Mars_PlayerCharacter_Hfsm_Inventory.as:187)). Equipping inside a crawl is a plausible bypass, not a reproduced exploit. Do not make it the foundation of a required puzzle yet.

## Acceptance observations, with explicit failure signals

These are proposed acceptance criteria, not published norms or existing results. Start with three unfamiliar pairs; have each repeat the same contract with roles swapped. Add the other two contract profiles after the first is complete. Small-sample results are design evidence, not a statistical claim.

| Check | Record / proposed pass signal | A failure changes |
|---|---|---|
| Shared world | In host/client sessions, both see the same route population, quarry, loot, door state and ingredient owner after either player acts. Swap host. **Zero state mismatches or missing required entities.** | Network/composition work; no pacing conclusions from an unequal world. |
| Recovery | Down each player once while carrying, climbing and separated by an unopened shortcut. **Zero irrecoverable essential-cargo states; a clear solo recovery or explicit failure/reset in every case.** | Recovery/ownership rules before harder encounters. |
| First orientation | Without a designer cue, each pair identifies the kitchen, operates its release and can point toward it from the Well. Record time, wrong turns and help requests; aim for **all three pairs without verbal intervention**. | Threshold, landmark and drive feedback, not another sign everywhere. |
| Useful choices | At least one consequential route decision and one optional reward decision per successful run. Players can explain the tradeoff afterward. | If they always choose shortest distance and cannot name a cost, change encounter/resource context. |
| Grotto alternatives | Across Grotto contracts, observe both approaches chosen for an articulated reason beyond novelty. Record extra travel and information gained. | Repurpose or simplify the longer branch if no recurring use appears. |
| Cooperation | Record each player's longest involuntary idle interval and task share. Proposed target: **no mandatory idle interval over 15 seconds**, and each player performs at least one consequential action in each major encounter. Observation and active guarding count; merely standing beside a safe holder does not. | Interaction sequencing, role exchange or removal of fake co-op. |
| Horror arc | After the run, each player names where anticipation began, where they felt most exposed, and where relief occurred. Those moments should broadly align with reveal -> commitment -> loaded return -> kitchen. | Cue timing, encounter rules and spacing; do not respond only with more darkness. |
| Counterplay | Deliberately try lantern aggro holding, threshold camping, dumping cargo ahead, blind Bellkeeper input and equip-after-crouch. Preserve clever bounded solutions; reject a repeatable zero-cost strategy that skips the encounter's decisions. | Specific rule causing the dominance, not blanket difficulty increases. |
| Dark navigation | Test the dimmest intended state with real player cameras: near ledges, exit direction, control reach and safe landing remain legible. The pair can lose detail without losing every available action. | Local contrast, silhouette and fixture placement; avoid globally washing out darkness. |
| Loaded movement | Each player carries the actual filled pack through both ladders, PK cargo door, both Grotto approaches and two-way passing situations. Test a dropped item at every one-way drop. **Zero unrecoverable jams/losses.** | Clearance or recovery, not graph connectivity. |
| Reward honesty | Every optional named puzzle has a guaranteed useful reward or visibly communicates that it is spent before the cost. Both can identify what Bellkeeper risks losing. | Cache policy and local presentation. |
| Run duration | Measure kitchen discovery, travel, active acquisition, handling, return and final cook separately for first and familiar runs. Report per-player activity and shared elapsed time; do not sum parallel work. | Short familiar runs call for better consequential actions, not padded tunnels or health. |

## Scope for the next iteration

2. Give that scenario one readable reveal, one meaningful route choice, one bounded cooperative encounter and one earned loaded return. Keep all required content dependable.
